# Key Findings: Costs and Harms of the Twice-Yearly Clock Change (Quick Reference)

*For use in synthesis on the House's July 14, 2026 passage of the Sunshine Protection Act (H.R. 139, 308-117).*

## Traffic Fatalities
- Fritz et al., *Current Biology* 2020 (732,835 fatal crashes, FARS data 1996-2017): fatal MVA risk rises **~6%** in the week after spring-forward, mainly morning crashes; ~**28 extra U.S. deaths/year** estimated.
- Same study: post-2007 Energy Policy Act (earlier March start date), the morning-crash spring effect **doubled**, from ~5-6% to **~13%**.
- Fritz et al. found **no significant fall-back effect** on fatal crash risk and no evidence DST reduces evening crashes via better light.
- A separate 2010-2019 FARS reanalysis found a higher **~12%** spring-week increase in occupant fatal crashes — estimates vary by dataset/method, but direction is consistent.
- Fall-back's real danger is different: earlier evening darkness raises pedestrian/cyclist fatal crashes **~13%** in following weeks; one estimate: **~171 avoidable pedestrian deaths/year** if fall clock change were eliminated. ~75-77% of pedestrian deaths already occur after dark.

## Cardiovascular Effects
- Janszky & Ljung, *NEJM* 2008 (Swedish MI registry, >100,000 cases): AMI incidence rate ratio **~1.05 (+5%)** in week after spring-forward; AMI incidence *fell* slightly after fall-back.
- Sandhu/Seth/Gurm, *Open Heart* 2014 ("Michigan study," 42,060 PCI admissions, 2010-2013): AMI admissions **~24-34% higher** the Monday after spring-forward vs. Monday before (93 vs. 125 across 4 years).
- Meta-analyses (2019-2020): spring AMI increase across studies ranges **~4% to 29%**; larger effect in women and adults under 65.
- Counterpoint: a large 2023/2024 U.S. probabilistic cardiovascular-outcomes study found **no robust net effect** on hospital deaths/strokes overall — evidence is strong but not unanimous.
- Stroke: Finnish study (Sipilä/Ruuskanen, 14,834 ischemic stroke patients, 2004-2013): hospitalization RR **~1.08 (+8%)** in first 2 days after any transition; **+25%** in cancer patients, **+20%** in those over 65.
- Atrial fibrillation: Montefiore study (~6,300 admissions, 2009-2016): AF admissions rose from 2.56/day baseline to **3.13/day (~+22%)** Mon-Thu after spring-forward; effect in women only; **no effect after fall-back**.

## Other Health/Safety Effects
- Mining/workplace injuries (Barnes & Wagner, *J. Applied Psychology* 2009; 576,292 mine injuries, 1983-2006): sleep loss of **~40 min** the Sunday of spring-forward (vs. ~12 min, not significant, in fall); workplace injuries **+5.7%** the following Monday; lost workdays from those injuries **+~68% ("67.6%")** in severity.
- Cyberloafing (Wagner/Ferris, Penn State, 6 years of Google data): non-work web use **+3.1%** the Monday after spring-forward vs. prior Monday, **+6.4%** vs. following Monday; **~8.4 extra minutes** of cyberloafing per lost hour of sleep in lab tests.
- Medical errors (Mayo Clinic data, *J. General Internal Medicine*, 2010-2017): patient-safety incidents **+18.7%** (significant) in week after spring-forward, mostly medication errors; fall-back increase (**+4.9%**) not statistically significant. Separate malpractice-claims study found **greater severity** of harm in claims after spring-forward.
- Suicide/mood: U.S. mortality analysis found **+6.25%** suicide rate and **+6.59%** combined suicide/substance-death rate after spring-forward; **no effect** after fall-back — implicates sleep loss, not daylight exposure, as mechanism.

## Economic Costs
- Chmura Economics "DST tax" / SleepBetter Lost-Hour Index: aggregate annual U.S. cost estimated at **~$434 million** (earlier estimate) rising to **~$670-672 million** (2024 update, added stroke costs) — a consultancy estimate aggregating peer-reviewed effect sizes, not itself peer-reviewed.
- Productivity: GitHub commit-activity study (Dickinson & Waddell, *J. Economic Behavior & Organization*) documents measurable post-transition productivity dips ("Sleepy Monday").
- Energy savings largely debunked: Kotchen & Grant (NBER WP 14429; *Rev. Econ. Statistics* 2011), 7M+ Indiana electricity meter-readings, 3 years: DST **increased** residential electricity use by **~1%** overall, **2-4% in fall**, because morning heating/afternoon AC demand outweighed reduced lighting demand. Cost to Indiana households: **~$9 million/year**; social/pollution cost: **~$1.7-5.5 million/year**.

## Critical Distinction: Switching Costs vs. Permanent-DST Costs
- Nearly all Sections above are costs of the **transition act itself** — eliminated by *either* permanent standard time or permanent DST.
- AASM position statement (*J. Clinical Sleep Medicine*, Jan. 2024, endorsed by 20+ organizations): U.S. should adopt **permanent standard time**, not permanent DST — standard time aligns with solar time/circadian biology year-round; permanent DST creates chronic "social jet lag" linked to long-run obesity, metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, depression risk.
- Under permanent DST, winter sunrise would be **after 8 a.m.** across much of the U.S. and **after 9 a.m.** in parts of the north/west of time zones — AASM's core objection.
- Historical precedent: the U.S. tried **permanent DST in 1974** (Nixon-era, oil-crisis motivated). Approval fell from **79% (Dec. 1973) to 42% (Feb. 1974)** amid reports of schoolchildren waiting at bus stops in the dark; **8 children killed in traffic accidents in Florida** in the weeks after the change; Congress repealed it and reverted to standard time by Oct. 1974 — the experiment lasted under a year.
- Current politics mirrors this split: House passed H.R. 139 (permanent **DST**) 308-117 on July 14, 2026; AASM "condemned" the vote (President Dr. Fariha Abbasi-Feinberg: House "ignored the overwhelming evidence supporting... permanent standard time"). AASM instead backs the competing **Sunshine for Our Kids Act (H.R. 9638)**, introduced July 9, 2026 (Reps. Scanlon D-PA / Harrigan R-NC), which would make **standard time** permanent. A prior Senate companion to the Sunshine Protection Act stalled after Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR) raised the same dark-winter-morning objection.

## Caveats for Synthesis Writer
- Point estimates vary meaningfully by study/dataset (e.g., 6% vs. 12% crash spike; 24% vs. 34% AMI spike) — present as ranges, cite the specific study for any single number used.
- The spring-transition sleep-loss effects (crashes, AMI, AF, suicide, errors) are consistently much stronger/more replicated than fall-transition effects on the *same* outcomes.
- Fall-transition harm operates through a different mechanism entirely (evening darkness → pedestrian risk), not sleep loss — don't conflate the two "sides" of the clock change.
- At least one large recent U.S. cardiovascular study found no robust net mortality effect — flag this as the field's most notable dissenting result.
- "Ending the clock changes" and "which permanent time is best" are separate, sometimes conflated debates — H.R. 139 answers the first question but resolves the second in the direction (permanent DST) that the sleep-medicine establishment says is the wrong one.
